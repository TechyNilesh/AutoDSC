\begin{abstract}
Data stream clustering must adapt to evolving distributions without ground-truth labels, so no single algorithm or hyperparameter configuration remains effective throughout a stream. Although AutoML has transformed supervised learning, automated algorithm selection and tuning for unsupervised data streams remain largely unexplored. We introduce AutoDSC (\underline{Auto}mated \underline{D}ata \underline{S}tream \underline{C}lustering), an AutoML framework that continuously searches the joint algorithm-and-hyperparameter space. Its \textit{Population-Guided Stochastic Search Optimisation} (PGSSO) combines a recency-weighted elite archive with adaptive exploration, while a composite of three internal cluster-validity indices enables label-free configuration selection. Across five real-world and five synthetic streams, AutoDSC achieves the best mean rank on both Adjusted Rand Index and Cluster Mapping Measure and remains computationally competitive with stream-clustering AutoML baselines.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
data stream clustering, AutoML, algorithm selection, unsupervised learning, multi-objective optimisation
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Data stream clustering is a fundamental operation across applications ranging from IoT sensor networks and network intrusion detection to social media analysis and financial monitoring~\cite{yang2022iot,bahri2021data}. Unlike batch clustering, stream clustering must process data continuously while adapting to distributions that change over time. Numerous stream-clustering algorithms have been proposed~\cite{aggarwal2003framework,Cao2006,Hahsler2016,montiel2021river}, each effective under particular conditions. However, the appropriate algorithm and hyperparameters may themselves change as the stream evolves, because no single configuration remains optimal throughout. Existing automated solutions rely on a single evaluation metric~\cite{carnein2020confstream}, require computationally intensive offline tuning~\cite{el2023onlineautoclust}, or reconfigure structural components without jointly searching the algorithm-and-hyperparameter space~\cite{wang2024mostream}. Together, these limitations leave a gap for a fully autonomous, label-free AutoML approach to stream clustering.

In this paper, we introduce AutoDSC (Automated Data Stream Clustering), a novel AutoML framework that automatically selects and tunes a clustering algorithm online as the data distribution evolves. We make the following key contributions.

\begin{itemize}
    \item We formalise data stream clustering as a combined algorithm selection and hyperparameter optimisation (CASH) problem~\cite{hutter2019automated} that must be solved repeatedly as the data distribution evolves, and present AutoDSC to solve this problem without labels or manual configuration.
    \item We introduce Population-Guided Stochastic Search Optimisation (PGSSO), which maintains a recency-weighted elite archive and an adaptively controlled exploration rate so that promising configurations are identified quickly and the population recovers implicitly when the distribution shifts.
    \item We design a normalised multi-objective quality measure combining multiple internal cluster-validity indices, providing robust label-free configuration selection.
    \item Across ten real-world and synthetic streams, we show that AutoDSC achieves the best mean rank on both quality metrics and the strongest throughput--memory balance among the evaluated AutoML frameworks.
\end{itemize}

Section~\ref{sec:methodology} presents the AutoDSC framework. Section~\ref{sec:setup} describes the experimental setup. Section~\ref{sec:results} presents the empirical evaluation and analysis, and Section~\ref{sec:conclusion_and_future} concludes the paper.

\section{Background}\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Data Stream Clustering}

A data stream is an unbounded, high-velocity sequence of instances that a learner must process continuously. A stream learner should inspect each instance at most once, use bounded time per instance and bounded memory, remain ready to produce an answer, and adapt to temporal change~\cite{bifet2018machine}. Because the data-generating distribution evolves, streams exhibit \textit{concept drift}: the number, shape, or location of clusters may change abruptly, gradually, or incrementally~\cite{bifet2018machine,aggarwal2007data}. A configuration that performs well at one point can therefore become suboptimal later.

Classical stream-clustering algorithms differ in how they summarise the stream. CluStream~\cite{aggarwal2003framework} maintains pyramidal time-frame statistics; density-based methods such as DenStream~\cite{Cao2006} and DBSTREAM~\cite{Hahsler2016} represent core, potential, and outlier micro-clusters; and k-means-based methods maintain centroids online, as in mini-batch web-scale clustering~\cite{Sculley2010} and StreamKMeans~\cite{montiel2021river}.

Stream-clustering quality is commonly evaluated with the prequential (interleaved test-then-train) protocol~\cite{bifet2018machine}, in which each instance tests the current model before updating it. Because ground-truth labels are unavailable during deployment, online selection relies on \textit{internal} cluster validity indices (CVIs), computed only from observations and cluster assignments. The Silhouette Coefficient~\cite{rousseeuw1987silhouettes} measures cohesion relative to neighbouring clusters; the Davies--Bouldin Index~\cite{davies1979cluster} compares intra-cluster scatter with inter-cluster separation; and the Calinski--Harabasz Index~\cite{calinski1974dendrite} compares between-cluster with within-cluster dispersion. By contrast, \textit{external} indices require labels and are reserved for evaluation. The Adjusted Rand Index (ARI)~\cite{hubert1985comparing,rand1971objective} measures chance-corrected agreement between predicted and true partitions, while the Cluster Mapping Measure (CMM)~\cite{kremer2011cmm} additionally accounts for emerging, splitting, and moving clusters in streams.

\subsection{Automated Machine Learning}

AutoML formalises algorithm selection and hyperparameter optimisation as the Combined Algorithm Selection and Hyperparameter Optimisation (CASH) problem~\cite{thornton2013autoweka,feurer2015autosklearn,hutter2019automated}. Common search methods include Bayesian optimisation, evolutionary and population-based methods, and random search~\cite{bergstra2012random,hansen2001cmaes}. Clustering-specific systems combine these methods with meta-learning or Bayesian search~\cite{Tschechlov2021AutoML4Clust,TrederTschechlov2023ML2DAC,ElShawi2021cSmartML,ElShawi2022TPEAutoClust}, but assume full access to a dataset drawn from a fixed distribution. AutoML has also been extended to supervised streams~\cite{pmlr-v139-wu21d,celik2023online,pmlr-v256-verma24a,verma2025auto-reg}; its application to unsupervised streams, where labels cannot guide the search, remains largely unexplored.

\subsection{Prior Work}\label{sec:relatedwork}

AutoML has been applied to batch clustering~\cite{Tschechlov2021AutoML4Clust,Liu2021AutoCluster,ElShawi2021cSmartML,TrederTschechlov2023ML2DAC,ElShawi2022TPEAutoClust,daSilva2024PoAC} and to supervised streams~\cite{celik2023online,pmlr-v256-verma24a,pmlr-v139-wu21d,verma2025auto-reg}, yet no existing system jointly addresses single-pass processing, label-free selection, and continuous distribution change. The closest work comprises three systems. ConfStream~\cite{carnein2020confstream} maintains an ensemble of configurations and resamples around better-performing members, but selects configurations using only Silhouette Width. OnlineAutoClust (OACE)~\cite{el2023onlineautoclust} applies tree-structured Parzen estimator Bayesian optimisation to clustering ensembles, but requires a computationally expensive offline tuning phase. MOStream~\cite{wang2024mostream}, building on the modular design space studied in SESAME~\cite{wang2023sesame}, reconfigures structural components by rule rather than searching a joint algorithm-and-hyperparameter space. AutoDSC instead combines single-pass population-guided search, a composite label-free selection criterion, and continual re-evaluation for implicit drift adaptation.

\section{Problem Formulation}\label{sec:problem}

A data stream is an unbounded sequence $\mathcal{S} = \{x_1, x_2, \ldots\}$ in which each instance $x_t \in \mathbb{R}^d$ arrives once. AutoDSC partitions the stream into consecutive, non-overlapping windows $W_s$ of fixed size $w$, where $s$ indexes stages. Because ground-truth labels are unavailable online, clustering quality on $W_s$ is estimated by an internal cluster-validity score $q(\cdot) \in [0,1]$ computed from observations and assignments alone; external indices are reserved for evaluation.

Let $\mathcal{P}$ be a portfolio of stream-clustering algorithms

\begin{equation}
\Theta = \bigcup_{a \in \mathcal{P}} (\{a\} \times \Phi_a)
\end{equation}

the joint space of configurations $\theta = (a, \phi)$ pairing an algorithm $a \in \mathcal{P}$ with hyperparameters $\phi \in \Phi_a$. The CASH problem for a stream is to track, at the end of each stage, the configuration

\begin{equation}
\theta_s^* = \arg\max_{\theta \in \Theta}\; q(\theta; W_s),
\end{equation}

using only the data observed through stage $s$ and bounded memory. Because drift can change $\theta_s^*$ over time, a stream CASH solver must repeatedly evaluate and select configurations.

\section{Automated Data Stream Clustering}\label{sec:methodology}

AutoDSC addresses the online CASH problem of Section~\ref{sec:problem} with an AutoML framework tailored to non-stationary data stream clustering. At each stage, it processes one window $W_s$ of $w$ instances and then evaluates the active configurations using multiple normalised validity indices. A recency-weighted archive of high-performing configurations guides subsequent population-based search. Figure~\ref{fig:framework_overview} illustrates this workflow.

\begin{figure*}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.92\linewidth]{images/diagrams/autodsc_framework.pdf}
    \caption{Overview of AutoDSC. At each stage, the configuration population is evaluated by a composite of internal validity indices, the incumbent is re-selected, and PGSSO proposes challengers using a recency-weighted elite archive and adaptive exploration.}
    \label{fig:framework_overview}
\end{figure*}

The framework maintains a population $\mathcal{A}_s \subseteq \Theta$ of algorithms with different hyperparameter settings and serves predictions from the current best-performing configuration, called the \textit{incumbent}. As each instance arrives, every population member first predicts its cluster and then updates incrementally with one \textsc{LearnOne} step. AutoDSC retains these pre-update assignments until the end of the stage for label-free scoring.

AutoDSC instantiates $q(\theta;W_s)$ as a composite of the normalised Silhouette Coefficient (NSC), Davies--Bouldin Index (NDBI), and Calinski--Harabasz Index (NCHI):

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{NSC} &= (1+\text{SC})/2, \\
\text{NDBI} &= 1-\tanh(\text{DBI}), \\
\text{NCHI} &= \tanh\!\left(\log(1+\text{CHI})/10\right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:normalisation}
\end{equation}

All three terms are bounded to $[0,1]$, with larger values indicating better quality. The composite score of configuration $\theta$ on $W_s$ is

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:score}
\mu_s(\theta) = \frac{1}{3}\bigl[\text{NSC}(\theta;W_s) + \text{NDBI}(\theta;W_s) + \text{NCHI}(\theta;W_s)\bigr].
\end{equation}

Equal weights avoid dataset-specific tuning and reduce dependence on any single CVI, whose reliability can vary with cluster shape and density~\cite{arbelaitz2013extensive,liu2010understanding}.

AutoDSC adjusts exploration from the change in the best population score, $\Delta\mu_s=\mu_{\text{best}}^{(s)}-\mu_{\text{prev}}$, where $\mu_{\text{best}}^{(s)}=\max_i\mu_s(\theta_i)$ and $\mu_{\text{prev}}$ is the preceding stage's best score. For stages after the first, the rate adapts as

\begin{equation}
\alpha_{s+1} = \begin{cases}
\max(0.1,\; \alpha_s(1-\beta_s)) & \Delta\mu_s > 0, \\
\min(1.0,\; \alpha_s(1+\beta_s)) & \Delta\mu_s \leq 0,
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\beta_s = \min(\gamma,\; |r_s|), \qquad
r_s = \frac{\Delta\mu_s}{|\mu_{\text{prev}}|},
\end{equation}

where $\gamma=0.5$ caps the update. At the first stage, AutoDSC keeps $\alpha_0=0.5$ and records $\mu_{\text{prev}}$. Thereafter, improvement reduces exploration, whereas stagnation or decline increases it and broadens the search.

The framework employs \textbf{Population-Guided Stochastic Search Optimisation (PGSSO)} (Algorithm~\ref{alg:pgsso}) to jointly search over algorithms and hyperparameters. PGSSO maintains an elite archive $\mathcal{E}_s=\{(\theta_i,z_i)\}_{i=1}^{e_{\max}}$, where $z_i$ is the score from the most recent evaluation of configuration $\theta_i=(a_i,\phi_i)$. Elites are ranked by $z_i/(1+\Delta_i)$, where $\Delta_i$ is the number of stages since the configuration was last evaluated. This recency weighting down-weights stale configurations. PGSSO then fits algorithm frequencies and, for each numerical hyperparameter $j$, a mean $m_j$ and variance $\sigma_j^2$ to guide sampling.

PGSSO generates new configurations through probabilistic sampling:

\begin{equation}
\theta_{\text{new}} = \begin{cases}
\text{Elite-Guided}(\mathcal{E}_s) & \text{with probability } 1-\alpha_s, \\
\text{Random}(\Theta) & \text{with probability } \alpha_s.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

Elite-guided sampling first draws an algorithm with probability equal to its frequency in the elite archive, then samples that algorithm's numerical hyperparameters from a Gaussian fitted to the elites using it:

\begin{equation}
\phi_j \sim \mathcal{N}(m_j, \sigma_j^2)
\end{equation}

where $m_j$ and $\sigma_j^2$ are the mean and variance of the $j$-th numerical hyperparameter computed over the elite configurations that use the selected algorithm (one Gaussian per hyperparameter dimension). Samples outside a hyperparameter's defined bounds are clipped to the boundary.

At each stage boundary, AutoDSC makes room for new challengers by retaining the incumbent, the highest-scoring configurations, and then configurations selected greedily by farthest-first distance. Configurations using different algorithms have distance $1$; otherwise, distance is the mean normalised difference across shared hyperparameters. The next population combines these retained configurations with newly instantiated challengers. Algorithm~\ref{alg:autodsc} gives the complete procedure.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{AutoDSC}
\label{alg:autodsc}
\KwIn{Stream $\mathcal{S} = \{x_1, x_2, \ldots\}$, $x_t \in \mathbb{R}^d$; window size $w$; population size $K$}
\KwOut{Cluster predictions $\{y_t\}_{t=1}^\infty$ emitted by the incumbent $\theta^*$}
$e_{\max} \gets \lfloor K/2 \rfloor$\;
$\mathcal{E} \gets \emptyset$, $\alpha \gets 0.5$, $\mu_{\text{prev}} \gets -\infty$, $s \gets 0$\;
$\mathcal{A}_0 \gets \{\theta_i\}_{i=1}^K$ where $\theta_i \sim \text{Uniform}(\Theta)$\;
$M_i \gets \textsc{Instantiate}(\theta_i)$ for each $i$\;
\tcc{$M_i$: online clusterer instantiated from $\theta_i = (a_i, \phi_i)$}
$\mathcal{A} \gets \mathcal{A}_0$, $W \gets \emptyset$, $\theta^* \gets \theta_1$\;
\ForEach{$x_t \in \mathcal{S}$}{
    $W \gets W \cup \{x_t\}$\;
    \ForEach{$\theta_i \in \mathcal{A}$}{
        $\ell_{i,t} \gets \textsc{PredictOne}(M_i, x_t)$\;
        $M_i \gets \textsc{LearnOne}(M_i, x_t)$\;
    }
    $y_t \gets \ell_{i^*,t}$ where $\theta_{i^*} = \theta^*$\;
    \tcc{emit prediction from current incumbent}
    \If{$|W| = w$}{
        $s \gets s+1$\;
        \ForEach{$\theta_i \in \mathcal{A}$}{
            $L_i \gets \{\ell_{i,\tau} : x_\tau \in W\}$\;
            $\mu_s(\theta_i) \gets \textsc{NormCVI}(W, L_i)$\;
        }
        $\mu_{\text{best}} \gets \max_i \mu_s(\theta_i)$\;
        $\theta^* \gets \arg\max_{\theta_i \in \mathcal{A}} \mu_s(\theta_i)$\;
        $\alpha \gets \textsc{Adapt}(\alpha,\mu_{\text{prev}},\mu_{\text{best}})$\;
        $\mu_{\text{prev}} \gets \mu_{\text{best}}$\;
        $(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{C}_{\text{new}}) \gets
        \text{PGSSO}(\mathcal{E},\{(\theta_i,\mu_s(\theta_i))\},\alpha,\Theta)$\;
        $\mathcal{A}_{\text{keep}} \gets
        \textsc{SelectDiverse}(\mathcal{A},K-|\mathcal{C}_{\text{new}}|)$\;
        $\mathcal{A} \gets \mathcal{A}_{\text{keep}} \cup
        \textsc{InstantiateAll}(\mathcal{C}_{\text{new}})$\;
        $W \gets \emptyset$\;
    }
}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{PGSSO}
\label{alg:pgsso}
\KwIn{Elite archive $\mathcal{E}$; scored configurations $\{(\theta_i,z_i)\}$; exploration rate $\alpha$; search space $\Theta$; population size $K$; archive capacity $e_{\max}$}
\KwOut{Updated archive $\mathcal{E}$ and challengers $\mathcal{C}_{\text{new}}$}
$n_{\text{gen}} \gets \min\!\bigl(\max(3,\lfloor e_{\max}/2\rfloor),K-1\bigr)$\;
$\mathcal{E} \gets \textsc{UpdateElitePopulation}(\mathcal{E},\{(\theta_i,z_i)\},e_{\max})$\;
\tcc{add/evict by recency-weighted score $z_i/(1+\Delta_i)$}
\textsc{UpdateParameterDistributions}($\mathcal{E}$)\;
\tcc{fit $\mathcal{N}(m_j,\sigma_j^2)$ per hyperparameter $j$ of each algorithm}
$\mathcal{C}_{\text{new}} \gets \emptyset$\;
\For{$c \gets 1$ \KwTo $n_{\text{gen}}$}{
    $u \gets \text{Uniform}(0,1)$\;
    \eIf{$u > \alpha$}{
        $\theta_{\text{new}} \gets \textsc{SampleFromElite}(\mathcal{E}, \Theta)$\;
    }{
        $\theta_{\text{new}} \gets \textsc{RandomSample}(\Theta)$\;
    }
    $\mathcal{C}_{\text{new}} \gets \mathcal{C}_{\text{new}} \cup \{\theta_{\text{new}}\}$\;
}
\Return $(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{C}_{\text{new}})$\;
\end{algorithm}

The five real-world streams are public benchmarks with natural class structure and distribution change.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Insects}~\cite{souza2020challenges}: optical-sensor wing-beat features of flying insects, where the species labels and feature distribution shift with ambient temperature over the recording.
\item \textbf{KDD99}~\cite{dua2019uci}: network-connection records from the KDD Cup 1999 intrusion-detection task, labelled as normal traffic or one of several attack types.
\item \textbf{PenDigits}~\cite{dua2019uci}: pen-trajectory features of handwritten digits, with one cluster per digit $0$--$9$.
\item \textbf{Shuttle}~\cite{dua2019uci}: NASA Space Shuttle radiator sensor readings (Statlog), with highly imbalanced operational-state classes.
\item \textbf{Vehicle}~\cite{duarte2004vehicle}: high-dimensional acoustic and seismic features from a distributed wireless sensor network, labelled by vehicle type.
\end{itemize}

The five synthetic streams isolate specific drift mechanisms under controlled conditions, holding dimensionality ($d{=}5$) and cluster count ($k{=}5$) fixed. Four are Gaussian-blob streams where the $i$-th cluster at time $t$ generates points $x \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{c}_i(t),\,\sigma_i(t)^2 I)$, differing only in how the centroid $\mathbf{c}_i(t) \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and spread $\sigma_i(t)$ evolve.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Expanding}: centroids are fixed ($\mathbf{c}_i(t){=}\mathbf{c}_i^0$); within-cluster standard deviation grows linearly, $\sigma_i(t) = \sigma_0 + \lambda t$, simulating continuous cluster expansion.
\item \textbf{Gradual}: each centroid translates at constant velocity along a fixed random direction, $\mathbf{c}_i(t) = \mathbf{c}_i^0 + v_i t$, producing smooth continuous drift throughout the stream.
\item \textbf{Merge-Split}: centroids move toward the global mean $\bar{\mathbf{c}}$ during the first half of the stream and symmetrically re-separate in the second half, producing a single merge-split event.
\item \textbf{Sudden}: centroid positions jump instantaneously at three evenly spaced change points and remain stationary between them, producing four stationary segments separated by abrupt concept drift.
\item \textbf{Fast-RBF}: a MOA RandomRBF stream~\cite{bifet2010moa} with a high centroid speed parameter, generating continuously drifting clusters with complex shapes and overlapping boundaries.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol}

AutoDSC, ConfStream, OACE, RandomSearch, and Default(Best) follow the prequential protocol. MOStream is evaluated through its released Benne interface, which in our environment requires a single fit over the standardised stream; following SESAME, its micro-clusters are then converted to macro-clusters by offline k-means refinement. We use ten independent runs per method and dataset to account for stochastic variability. Clustering quality is measured by ARI~\cite{hubert1985comparing} and CMM~\cite{kremer2011cmm}; ground-truth labels are used only for evaluation, never for AutoDSC training or configuration selection. We also record throughput (instances/s) and memory usage (MB). Statistical significance is assessed with the Friedman test followed by the Nemenyi post-hoc procedure~\cite{demvsar2006statistical} at $p=0.05$.

\subsection{Baseline Methods Selection}
We compare AutoDSC against five baselines spanning three categories.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{AutoML:} ConfStream~\cite{carnein2020confstream} and OACE~\cite{el2023onlineautoclust}, established frameworks that optimise algorithm selection and hyperparameters online.
    \item \textbf{Rule-based:} MOStream~\cite{wang2024mostream}, which reconfigures structural components by rule without searching the joint algorithm-and-hyperparameter space.
    \item \textbf{Baselines:} RandomSearch~\cite{bergstra2012random}, which shares AutoDSC's framework structure but generates configurations by purely random sampling, and Default(Best)~\cite{el2023onlineautoclust}, which runs each base algorithm from the search space (Table~\ref{tab:hyperparameters}) at its default hyperparameters and reports the best-performing one.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Sensitivity Analysis}\label{sec:sensitivity}

Figure~\ref{fig:sensitivity_study} reports AutoDSC's sensitivity to its two free hyperparameters, window size $w$ and population size $K$, averaged over all datasets. For window size, quality peaks at $w = 1000$, where smaller stages give insufficient data for reliable per-stage CVI evaluation and larger stages delay adaptation to drift and reduce throughput; the quality curve is thus unimodal with a clear optimum. For population size, both ARI and CMM rise steeply from small $K$ and saturate around $K \approx 15$, while throughput falls monotonically, so the default $K = 10$ sits at the knee of the quality-efficiency trade-off. Overall, AutoDSC is robust within a broad range of both parameters and degrades gracefully outside it, with the default configuration ($w=1000$, $K=10$) providing the best balance between clustering quality and computational efficiency.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}
AutoDSC demonstrates that label-free, single-pass AutoML is feasible and competitive for evolving data streams, while the PGSSO search itself contributes little overhead relative to base-clusterer training. The composite scoring function reduces dependence on any one internal validity index~\cite{arbelaitz2013extensive,liu2010understanding}, and adaptive exploration broadens the search when the incumbent score stagnates or declines. AutoDSC's clearest advantages are autonomy and computational efficiency, together with significant ARI gains over untuned algorithms and random search. Its value is therefore best framed as delivering strong quality automatically rather than winning uniformly on every dataset and metric.

Several limitations bound the scope of our claims. First, with ten datasets, AutoDSC's advantage over the strongest AutoML competitors, ConfStream and OACE, is not statistically significant, although AutoDSC has the best mean rank and significantly outperforms untuned algorithms and random search on ARI. Second, the evaluation targets numerical tabular streams; sparse text, multimodal, and image streams would require domain-specific base clusterers. Third, AutoDSC exposes two framework-level hyperparameters, window size and population size, although the sensitivity analysis shows stability across broad ranges. Fourth, while PGSSO supports categorical parameters, the current portfolio contains only numerical and integer choices. Finally, internal validity indices estimate rather than observe clustering quality online, so a misleading score can temporarily affect configuration selection until later stages provide new evidence.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}\label{sec:conclusion_and_future}
We presented AutoDSC, a single-pass AutoML framework that uses PGSSO to propose stream-clustering configurations and a composite internal criterion to select among them without labels. Across ten streams, AutoDSC achieves the best mean rank on ARI and CMM, significantly outperforms untuned algorithms and random search on ARI, and remains computationally competitive with AutoML stream clusterers. These results show that continuous, label-free algorithm selection and hyperparameter optimisation are feasible for evolving streams. Future work will investigate partial label feedback, richer selection objectives, and portfolios with categorical structural choices.
